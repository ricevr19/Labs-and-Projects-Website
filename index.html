Welcome: Welcome to Evidence-Based, Hands-On Technical Learning Learning by doing (not watching, not passively listening). Reasoning like an IT professional/analyst. How This Course Works: Executio Build real systems. Execute tasks. Evidence Collect evidence. Reasoning Analyze your work. Explain your reasoning. Submit professional reports.
How This Course Differs from Traditional Courses: Traditional Course This Course Lectures/Slides Short focused introductions (web page) Memorizing facts Applying concepts directly Pre-canned labs Practical full-stack configurations High-level exercises Deep hands-on troubleshooting Limited reportingStructured LaTeX-based reporting “Guess the answer” questions Evidence-based reasoning questions What You Will Do: Work in teams fully collaborating. Execute full labs with real configurations. Collect evidence (screenshots, logs, packet captures). Answer reasoning questions linked to your execution. Report your reasoning by answering questions supported with evidence Submit structured reports in LaTeX. How Reasoning Is Assessed: Reasoning is central: You must connect **what you did** (execution), **what you observed** (evidence) to **why** (reasoning) and substantiate . Questions are not answered from memory but from troubleshooting and analyzing your execution results. Professional reporting habits are emphasized for objective grading. Why This Approach Helps You: Builds real-world problem-solving skills. Teaches applied learning by doing (not watching). Develops critical troubleshooting abilities. Prepares you for professional structured technical reporting. Provides reasoning feedback and critical thinking habits. The Role of Learning Exercises (LEs): Weekly reasoning practice based on labs. Immediate feedback with opportunity for corrections (2 attempts). Reinforces concepts for labs and exams. How To Succeed in This Course Start Early: Labs are multi-stage — don't leave anything for the last minute. Work With Your Partner(s): Collaborate, don’t just split tasks. Collect Evidence Thoughtfully: Screenshots and data are your proof. Pay attention to execution + analysis: Don't shortcut reasoning. Think Before You Write: Reasoning is more important than word count. Be concise and write to the point: using your collected evidence. Use the Lab Report Templates: The LaTeX format guides your thinking and reporting. Engage in Learning Exercises (LEs): They're to re-enforce hands-on learnings. Reflect on Feedback: Each report is a chance to improve — not just a grade. Ask questions and seek help: Every step of the way, reach out to your instructor/TAs with questions. Grading and Feedback Grading is honest and rubric-based. Grades are curved fairly based on the cohort performance distribution. Feedback uses both structured comments and abbreviations. Referencing evidence and external citations is a must. Reasoning quality and conciseness matter the most. Bottom line: Learn how real technical professionals solve problems: Execute - Analyze - Support with evidence - Explain your reasoning. Also, it is referred to, in this course, as Execution-Evidence-Reasoning (EER) approach. This course doesn’t just teach you networking — it teaches you how to think, analyze, and communicate like a technical analyst professional. You will work hard — and you will walk away with practical and transferable skills.
